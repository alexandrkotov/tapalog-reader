# Scanned books

A PDF made of page pictures has no text. This is how the program reads it, how the text is checked and corrected, how figures are cut out, how a new PDF is made, and how the words become selectable over the scan.

## Recognizing scanned books

Administrator only, optional; needs search inside books on. A PDF made of page pictures has no text, so the program cannot search it. **Recognize scanned pages…** in Settings lists the PDFs without text (and a PDF's own card has a button for it), and recognizes the text of the ticked ones **in the administrator's browser** with [Tesseract](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js) (tesseract.js, Apache-2.0; the engine and the Russian and English models are inside the program, about 10 MB, nothing is downloaded and nothing leaves the machine). Choose Russian, English or both; pages are drawn by pdf.js, recognized on up to four lanes at once (about 1.5 s a page on a modern computer with four lanes, 3–12 s on one core), and each page is sent to the server as soon as it is recognized, so a stop or a closed window loses only the pages in hand and a second run skips the pages already done. The window must stay open while it works. The recognized text is kept in `fulltext.db` apart from the text of the index (it is not lost when the index is rebuilt, and **Forget** removes it for a book), and search finds it beside the rest, with the page number and the text around the words. The list in the window can be searched by title or folder, filtered by state (all, not recognized, half done, recognized) and ordered (by folder, by title, the unrecognized first, the biggest files first; the order is remembered), and **Select the unrecognized** ticks only the books on view. Needs a browser with WebAssembly SIMD (every current one). The file is never changed. The words can then be selected and copied in the reader (see the end of this page).

## Checking and correcting the text

Administrator only: **Check the text…** in the window for recognizing scans, or **Check the text beside the scan…** on a PDF's card, opens a window in which every page is one row, the picture of the scan on the left and its text on the right, in one list that scrolls as a whole, so the two never part. Pictures are drawn only near the window, so a book of a thousand pages costs no more than a screenful. The text can be corrected on the spot: a page is saved by itself a second after the last key (or at once when the cursor leaves it or the window is closed, and again after a failure of the network), a corrected page is marked and the recognizer never replaces it, **Reset to recognized** takes a page back to what was read, and a field takes you to a page by its number. Search finds the corrected words at once. A book never recognized can be written by hand the same way (nothing is stored until the first page is saved). **Download Markdown** and **Download text** save the book as one file with the number of every page (and a note saying how many pages are recognized, if not all); the language of the page headings follows the language of the interface. **Forget the text** deletes the corrections too, and says how many pages that is before it does. The PDF file is never changed

## The visual editor for the text of a page

The text of a page is edited the way it will read: **bold** and *italic* text, headings (type `## ` before a line), lists, quotes and formulas are drawn in place, and the figures cut from the scan show as pictures. Formulas are written in LaTeX between dollar signs (`$E=mc^2$` in a line, `$$ … $$` as a block of its own) and drawn by KaTeX; a dollar sign is a formula only when no space follows it, no space comes before the closing one and no digit after it, so "costs $5 and $6" stays text. **Enter** starts a paragraph and **Shift+Enter** a new line of the same paragraph (a line break of the text is a line break, as it always was); Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I and Ctrl+Z work as everywhere. The row of tools above the column of the text (undo and redo, bold, italic, code, heading, bulleted and numbered list, quote, and two formula buttons) acts on the page whose text the cursor is in, and is off in the plain text. **↶** and **↷** (the same as Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z) take back and bring back one change at a time; the history of a page is kept while its editor is on screen, so a page scrolled far away and back starts with an empty history (its text is not lost). That is not what **Reset to recognized** in the heading of a page does: it throws away all the changes to the page at once, after asking. **∑** opens a window for a formula: LaTeX in a field, a drawing of it that follows what is typed, a few pieces to put in (fraction, root, power, sum, integral, Greek letters, comparison signs), a choice of a line of its own, and **Ctrl+Enter** to insert; a click on a formula in the text opens the same window to change or delete it, and **Esc** there closes only that window. **Source text** in the bar of the window shows the plain text of every page in a field instead of the editor (and back); the choice is remembered in the browser, and the text is kept the same either way: it is Markdown, and only the figures `![Fig. 1.1](images/p001-1.png)`, bold, headings, lists, quotes and formulas in it mean anything.

A page is rewritten from the editor only after it was changed in it: opening a page, clicking into its text or leaving it saves nothing, so the text the recognizer read stays exactly as it was. A page that was changed is written back as plain lines; the signs that the editor protects (`3 \* 4`, `\[1]`) are written plainly again. The editor is a script of about 0.7 MB that the page fetches from the program when the window is opened for the first time (it is not part of the page itself), and it is made only for the pages near the window, so a book of a thousand pages costs no more than a screenful. If it cannot be loaded, the plain text is shown instead.

## The size of the window and of the page

The window for checking the text can be made bigger: the button beside the **×** (or a double click on the title) makes it almost as big as the screen, with a space of 24 pixels left around it, so that it stays a window over the page, and the same again takes it back. Its edges and corners can be dragged to any size between 640 × 420 pixels and that biggest one; the window stays in the middle, and the edge that is held follows the pointer. The size is kept in the browser (`tapalog.proofSize`) and the scans are drawn again for the room they have, so they stay sharp; the list stays at the same place of the book while the size changes. On a narrow screen, where the window is as big as the screen already, there are no handles.

**Fit the page** in the bar of the window shows every page whole: the scan is as tall as the list lets it be (with the page's own proportions, in the middle of its column) and the text beside it as tall as the scan, so nothing has to be scrolled to see the foot of a page. The choice is kept in the browser (`tapalog.proofFit`). On a narrow screen, where the scan stands above its text, the switch is not shown. Neither of them changes the PDF file.

## Figures from the scan

Administrator only, in the same window: **Select a figure** turns on a mode in which a frame dragged on the scan (with the mouse or a finger) cuts that part of the page out of the PDF, drawn again from the file at up to 4 pixels per point (no more than 3200 on the longer side), so it is sharper than the page on the screen. The picture is a PNG kept in `fulltext.db` with the book's text (up to 12 MB and 1000 pictures a book), named after the page and a number (`p008-1.png`), and its reference `![Fig. 8.1](images/p008-1.png)` is put into the text of that page as a paragraph of its own where the cursor is. Small pictures of a page's figures are shown under its heading: **Insert** puts one into the text again, **×** deletes it together with every reference to it in the text. The references are not indexed, so search never finds "images" or the file names. The same goes for the marks of the editor: a page that was edited is indexed without its `**`, `#` and `>` and without its formulas (their LaTeX is not words to look for), and **Download text** is plain too (a formula stays as its LaTeX, without the dollar signs), while **Download Markdown** keeps everything as it was typed. **Download Markdown with figures (.zip)** (shown when the book has figures) gives `book.md` and an `images/` folder with the figures the text refers to; **Download text** leaves the references out. **Forget the text** says how many figures will be lost. **Page as a figure** in the heading of every page cuts the whole page as one figure (no frame to draw, in a window of any size), at the same resolution limits. While a frame is dragged, the list scrolls by itself when the pointer is near the top or bottom edge of it (faster the nearer, and beyond it), so a figure that reaches below what is in sight is made in one drag. A picture over 11 MB (a whole page of a colour scan can be) is cut again, 30% smaller each time but not below one pixel per point, instead of being refused by the server. The PDF file is never changed

## A new PDF from the checked book

Administrator only, in the same window: **Export to a new PDF** opens a line with the choice of the sheet (A4 or A5) and of the headings ("Page 8" above the text of each page), and **Make the PDF** sets the text and the figures on the sheets and downloads the file, named after the book. The PDF is written by the browser (pdf-lib and its font reader, MIT, fetched from the program's own files only when a PDF is made, so they do not weigh on the page; the letters are DejaVu Sans, which has the Russian, Ukrainian and English alphabets, and only the letters used are kept in the file), so its text can be selected and searched, but it is a new typeset document, not a copy of the scan. The text is taken from the server after everything typed has been sent, a line break typed by hand stays a line break and a blank line parts paragraphs, the figures stand where their references are, at the size they have for paper and no wider than the text (the picture is shrunk to about 200 pixels per inch and kept as PNG, or as JPEG when that is less than 40% of the PNG: photographs and halftones), and a figure that does not fit under the text moves to the next sheet whole. The marks of the text are set, not printed: bold and italic words (the font has one weight and no slant, so bold is drawn with an outline and italic is slanted), headings larger and bold, lists with a bullet or their number, quotes indented and grey; a formula is printed as its LaTeX in italics (the letters of the font have no mathematical signs, so it is not drawn as KaTeX draws it), a formula of its own on a line of its own. A note says how many pages are recognized when not all are, and every sheet has its number. The choices are kept in the browser. The file is only downloaded: nothing goes into the library, and the scan is never changed

## Books with pages of both kinds

Administrator only, in the same window: a PDF often has some pages typeset (they have text in the file) and others scanned. When a book is recognized, a page that already has enough text of its own (25 letters or more; a page with only a number or a stamp is read) is taken as it is, without being drawn or read, and only the pictures are read. The checkbox **Take the pages that have their own text in the PDF as they are, and read only the pages that are pictures** is on by default and remembered; with it off every page is read from its picture. The row of a book says how many of its pages came from the PDF, and the result line how many were taken in the run. These pages are part of the book for checking, correcting and export (the text of the file is what is shown; **Reset to recognized** brings it back after a correction), are searched once through the index of the file, not twice, and have no words over the picture, because the reader selects their own text itself. The lines of such a page may come as one line, the way the index reads them. A book is offered for recognizing when it has little text in all (under 2000 letters), when at least 3 of its pages (and a tenth of the book) have no text of their own, or when a run began on it. The index counts the pages of every PDF and the pages that have text; books indexed before are measured once in the background, without touching the search. The row of such a book says "N of M pages have no text" until it is done, and the list can be ordered by the number of pages (**Order: most pages first**).

## Selectable text over a scan

Every reader sees it: the words the recognizer found on a page lie over the picture in no colour, each as wide and high as its frame on the page, so a word can be selected with a double click, a line or a page dragged over and copied (the lines come out in reading order), and the browser's own search finds it. The words follow the turning and the cropping of the pages and stay out of the way of the area tool. The program keeps the frames of the words beside the text of each page (about 5 KB a page) and gives a reader only the pages that have them, one request for the list and one for each page seen; the PDF itself is never changed. A page you wrote by hand has none (its words are no longer the found ones), and taking your writing back brings them again. Books recognized before this have the text but not the frames: the window for recognizing scans says how many pages of a book lack them (**N pages without selectable text**), **Select those without selectable text** ticks such books and turns on **Also read again the pages recognized before**, and a run with that on reads again only those pages, never one you corrected. The readers of the recognition (the engine's workers) are made once for a run of several books and ended with it

# Scanned books

A PDF made of page pictures has no text. This is how the program reads it, how the text is checked and corrected, how figures are cut out, how a new PDF is made, and how the words become selectable over the scan.

## Recognizing scanned books

Administrator only, optional; needs search inside books on. A PDF made of page pictures has no text, so the program cannot search it. **Recognize scanned pages…** in Settings lists the PDFs without text (and a PDF's own card has a button for it), and recognizes the text of the ticked ones **in the administrator's browser** with [Tesseract](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js) (tesseract.js, Apache-2.0; the engine and the Russian and English models are inside the program, about 10 MB, nothing is downloaded and nothing leaves the machine). Choose Russian, English or both; pages are drawn by pdf.js, recognized on up to four lanes at once (about 1.5 s a page on a modern computer with four lanes, 3–12 s on one core), and each page is sent to the server as soon as it is recognized, so a stop or a closed window loses only the pages in hand and a second run skips the pages already done. The window must stay open while it works. The recognized text is kept in `fulltext.db` apart from the text of the index (it is not lost when the index is rebuilt, and **Forget** removes it for a book), and search finds it beside the rest, with the page number and the text around the words. The list in the window can be searched by title or folder, filtered by state (all, not recognized, half done, recognized) and ordered (by folder, by title, the unrecognized first, the biggest files first; the order is remembered), and **Select the unrecognized** ticks only the books on view. Needs a browser with WebAssembly SIMD (every current one). The file is never changed. The words can then be selected and copied in the reader (see the end of this page).

## Checking and correcting the text

Administrator only: **Check the text…** in the window for recognizing scans, or **Check the text beside the scan…** on a PDF's card, opens a window in which every page is one row, the picture of the scan on the left and its text on the right, in one list that scrolls as a whole, so the two never part. Pictures are drawn only near the window, so a book of a thousand pages costs no more than a screenful. The text can be corrected on the spot: a page is saved by itself a second after the last key (or at once when the cursor leaves it or the window is closed, and again after a failure of the network), a corrected page is marked and the recognizer never replaces it, **Restore the recognized text** takes a page back to what was read, and a field takes you to a page by its number. Search finds the corrected words at once. A book never recognized can be written by hand the same way (nothing is stored until the first page is saved). **Download Markdown** and **Download text** save the book as one file with the number of every page (and a note saying how many pages are recognized, if not all); the language of the page headings follows the language of the interface. **Forget the text** deletes the corrections too, and says how many pages that is before it does. The PDF file is never changed

## Figures from the scan

Administrator only, in the same window: **Select a figure** turns on a mode in which a frame dragged on the scan (with the mouse or a finger) cuts that part of the page out of the PDF, drawn again from the file at up to 4 pixels per point (no more than 3200 on the longer side), so it is sharper than the page on the screen. The picture is a PNG kept in `fulltext.db` with the book's text (up to 12 MB and 1000 pictures a book), named after the page and a number (`p008-1.png`), and its reference `![Fig. 8.1](images/p008-1.png)` is put into the text of that page as a paragraph of its own where the cursor is. Small pictures of a page's figures are shown under its heading: **Insert** puts one into the text again, **×** deletes it together with every reference to it in the text. The references are not indexed, so search never finds "images" or the file names. **Download Markdown with figures (.zip)** (shown when the book has figures) gives `book.md` and an `images/` folder with the figures the text refers to; **Download text** leaves the references out. **Forget the text** says how many figures will be lost. The PDF file is never changed

## A new PDF from the checked book

Administrator only, in the same window: **Export to a new PDF** opens a line with the choice of the sheet (A4 or A5) and of the headings ("Page 8" above the text of each page), and **Make the PDF** sets the text and the figures on the sheets and downloads the file, named after the book. The PDF is written by the browser (pdf-lib and its font reader, MIT, fetched from the program's own files only when a PDF is made, so they do not weigh on the page; the letters are DejaVu Sans, which has the Russian, Ukrainian and English alphabets, and only the letters used are kept in the file), so its text can be selected and searched, but it is a new typeset document, not a copy of the scan. The text is taken from the server after everything typed has been sent, a line break typed by hand stays a line break and a blank line parts paragraphs, the figures stand where their references are, at the size they have for paper and no wider than the text (the picture is shrunk to about 200 pixels per inch and kept as PNG, or as JPEG when that is less than 40% of the PNG: photographs and halftones), and a figure that does not fit under the text moves to the next sheet whole. A note says how many pages are recognized when not all are, and every sheet has its number. The choices are kept in the browser. The file is only downloaded: nothing goes into the library, and the scan is never changed

## Selectable text over a scan

Every reader sees it: the words the recognizer found on a page lie over the picture in no colour, each as wide and high as its frame on the page, so a word can be selected with a double click, a line or a page dragged over and copied (the lines come out in reading order), and the browser's own search finds it. The words follow the turning and the cropping of the pages and stay out of the way of the area tool. The program keeps the frames of the words beside the text of each page (about 5 KB a page) and gives a reader only the pages that have them, one request for the list and one for each page seen; the PDF itself is never changed. A page you wrote by hand has none (its words are no longer the found ones), and taking your writing back brings them again. Books recognized before this have the text but not the frames: the window for recognizing scans says how many pages of a book lack them (**N pages without selectable text**), **Select those without selectable text** ticks such books and turns on **Also read again the pages recognized before**, and a run with that on reads again only those pages, never one you corrected. The readers of the recognition (the engine's workers) are made once for a run of several books and ended with it
